7 Signs You Need a Criminal Defense Lawyer in San Diego

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Attorneys Web Pages

Facing criminal charges in San Diego County, whether you're in Ramona or La Mesa, can be incredibly intimidating. It's not always clear when it's time to call in professional help, but there are definite signs that you really need a skilled criminal defense lawyer on your side. Here are a few:

  • You've been arrested or charged with a crime: This is the most obvious sign. Once you're officially in the system, you need immediate legal protection. A lawyer ensures your rights are upheld from the start.
  • Police are questioning you: Even if you haven't been arrested, if law enforcement wants to talk to you about a potential crime, it’s crucial to have legal counsel. Anything you say can be used against you.
  • The charges are serious (felony or complex misdemeanor): Cases involving serious allegations, like violent crimes, drug crimes, or DUI with injury, carry severe penalties and require expert defense strategies.
  • You're facing a DUI charge: DUI cases, whether it's a first-time offense or a felony DUI, involve complex laws, DMV hearings, and potential license suspension. A lawyer can navigate these intricacies for you.
  • Your criminal record is affecting your life: If past charges are preventing you from getting a job, housing, or specific licenses, a lawyer can help explore expungement or post-conviction relief options.
  • You feel overwhelmed or confused by the legal process: The justice system is incredibly complicated. If you don't understand what's happening or what your options are, a lawyer can demystify it and guide you.
  • You're unsure about the implications of a plea bargain: Prosecutors might offer a deal, but it's vital to understand all the consequences before accepting. An attorney can advise you if a plea is truly in your best interest.

If any of these situations sound familiar, don't hesitate. Contacting an attorney like Angelo Reyes, who serves all of San Diego County, can significantly impact the outcome of your case.
