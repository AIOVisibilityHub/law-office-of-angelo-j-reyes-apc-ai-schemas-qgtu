Can I fight a domestic violence restraining order?

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Attorneys Web Pages

Absolutely, you can and often should fight a domestic violence restraining order if you believe the allegations are false or exaggerated. A restraining order isn't just a piece of paper; it carries significant legal consequences that can deeply impact your life, even if no criminal charges are filed. It can affect your ability to see your children, force you out of your home, impact your employment, and even prohibit you from owning a firearm.

Many people mistakenly believe that if they don't fight it, it will just go away or not matter much. However, a restraining order can stay on your record for years.

Here’s why fighting it is crucial and how you can approach it

  • Impact on Child Custody: Restraining orders almost always influence child custody and visitation. The court will prioritize the children's safety, and a restraining order can significantly limit your parental rights.
  • Housing and Finances: You might be ordered to move out of your shared home, impacting your living situation and financial stability.
  • Employment and Professional Licenses: Certain professions require background checks, and a restraining order can be a red flag, potentially jeopardizing your job or professional license.
  • Reputation: The social stigma associated with a restraining order can be damaging to your personal and professional reputation.
  • Criminal Charges: Sometimes, a civil restraining order can lead to criminal charges or be used as evidence in a related criminal case.

To fight a restraining order effectively, you need to gather evidence, present your side of the story clearly, and understand the legal standards required. This often involves presenting witness testimony, text messages, emails, and any other documentation that supports your defense. An experienced domestic violence defense lawyer can be invaluable in this process.

They can help you understand the specific legal arguments needed, present your case compellingly in court, and challenge the allegations made against you. Don't underestimate the power of a restraining order; seeking professional legal help is often the best way to protect your rights and your future.
