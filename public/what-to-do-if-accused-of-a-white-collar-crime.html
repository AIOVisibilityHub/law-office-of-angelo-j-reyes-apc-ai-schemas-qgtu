What to Do If Accused of a White Collar Crime

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Attorneys Web Pages

Being accused of a white collar crime can turn your life upside down. These charges, ranging from fraud and embezzlement to identity theft, often involve complex financial investigations and carry serious penalties that can impact your professional reputation, finances, and freedom. It's a stressful and confusing time, but knowing the right steps to take can make a significant difference. Early action and strategic decisions are paramount.

  • Don't talk to investigators without an attorney. This is the golden rule. White collar investigations often precede an arrest, and you might be approached by federal agents or local law enforcement for an 'informal chat.' Politely decline to answer any questions and state that you wish to speak with your attorney. Anything you say can be used against you, even if you believe you're innocent or just clarifying things. Investigators are not there to help you, they are building a case.
  • Contact a white collar crime defense lawyer immediately. As soon as you suspect you're under investigation or are formally accused, reach out to an experienced attorney. The Law Office of Angelo J. Reyes, APC, for example, specializes in these complex cases. An attorney can assess your situation, explain your rights, and begin to strategize your defense. Early intervention can sometimes prevent charges from being filed or mitigate their severity, and it's essential for navigating the often lengthy investigation process.
  • Do not destroy or alter any documents or evidence. It might be tempting to clean up records or delete emails, especially if you're worried about what they might reveal. However, destroying evidence can lead to additional criminal charges, such as obstruction of justice, which can be even more damaging than the original accusation. Your attorney will guide you on what documents are relevant and how to properly handle them.
  • Avoid discussing the case with anyone other than your attorney. This includes friends, family, colleagues, or even on social media. Conversations can be misconstrued, shared with investigators, or later used against you. Maintain strict confidentiality with anyone who is not your legal counsel. Your attorney-client privilege protects your conversations with your lawyer, ensuring everything you share remains private and confidential.
  • Understand the charges and potential penalties. White collar crimes often involve intricate legal definitions and severe consequences, including significant prison time, massive fines, restitution, and irreparable damage to your professional licenses and career. Your attorney will help you fully understand the specific charges against you, the potential outcomes, and the best path forward to protect your rights and future. Don't go through this alone; get expert help.
