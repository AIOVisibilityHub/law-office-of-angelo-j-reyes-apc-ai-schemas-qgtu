What to Do If You're Facing a Federal Investigation

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Attorneys Web Pages

Getting word that you're under federal investigation can send chills down your spine. Unlike local police, federal agencies like the FBI, DEA, or IRS often conduct long, detailed investigations before any charges are even filed. This pre-charge phase is incredibly important, and how you react can significantly impact the outcome of your case. It’s not just about what you do, but what you absolutely don't do.

Here are some key mistakes to avoid if you find yourself in this daunting situation:

  • Don't talk to federal agents without legal counsel. It's natural to want to explain your side, but anything you say can and will be used against you. Federal agents are highly skilled interrogators. Politely state that you wish to speak with an attorney before answering any questions. Avoiding this mistake: Insist on having your lawyer present. Your right to counsel is paramount, and Attorney Reyes offers direct access to your attorney, not an intake line.
  • Don't destroy or alter any documents or electronic data. This includes emails, texts, financial records, and hard drives. Tampering with evidence is a serious federal offense that can lead to additional charges and severely weaken your defense. Avoiding this mistake: Preserve everything, even if you think it might look bad. Let your attorney sort through what's relevant and admissible.
  • Don't try to contact co-defendants or witnesses about the case. Reaching out to others involved could be perceived as obstruction of justice or witness tampering, which are also serious federal crimes. It’s crucial to let your attorney manage all communications related to the investigation. Avoiding this mistake: Keep a distance from anyone else connected to the case and refer all inquiries to your lawyer.
  • Don't assume your case is too small for federal attention. Federal agencies have broad jurisdiction, and what might seem like a minor offense to you could be part of a larger federal investigation. Federal charges often carry much harsher penalties than state charges. Avoiding this mistake: Take every federal inquiry seriously and seek expert legal advice immediately, even if you think it's a misunderstanding.
  • Don't delay seeking experienced legal representation. Federal cases are incredibly complex, with unique procedures, rules of evidence, and sentencing guidelines. A lawyer who specializes in federal criminal defense will understand these intricacies. Avoiding this mistake: Contact a federal criminal defense attorney like Angelo J. Reyes right away. Early intervention can make a significant difference, potentially even preventing charges from being filed in the first place.
