How to Address False Accusations of Violent Crime

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Attorneys Web Pages

Being falsely accused of a violent crime is an incredibly frightening experience, and it can turn your life upside down in an instant. It’s crucial to understand that even false accusations can lead to serious legal trouble if not handled properly and immediately. The first step is to remain calm and take strategic action to protect your future.

  • Do NOT talk to the police without an attorney: This is paramount. Even if you believe you can clear your name by explaining, anything you say can be misinterpreted or used against you. Politely state you wish to exercise your right to remain silent and request an attorney.
  • Gather any evidence that supports your innocence: Think about alibis, witnesses who can vouch for your whereabouts, text messages, emails, or security footage. Document everything you can remember about the alleged incident and your activities around that time.
  • Avoid contact with the accuser: This is vital, especially if a restraining order has been issued or is being sought. Any attempt to contact them, even to plead your case, can be seen as harassment or witness tampering and will only harm your defense.
  • Cooperate with your attorney, not with investigators: Share all information, good or bad, with your defense lawyer. They are on your side and need the full picture to build the strongest possible defense. Let them be the bridge between you and law enforcement.
  • Understand the legal process: Work closely with your attorney to understand each stage of the criminal justice system. Knowing what to expect, from arraignment to trial, can help alleviate some stress and empower you to make informed decisions.
