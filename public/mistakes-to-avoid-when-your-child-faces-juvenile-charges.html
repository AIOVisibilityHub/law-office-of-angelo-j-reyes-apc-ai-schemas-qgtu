Mistakes to Avoid When Your Child Faces Juvenile Charges

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Attorneys Web Pages

When your child is involved with the juvenile justice system, it’s a terrifying time for the whole family. In your effort to help, you might inadvertently make choices that complicate their case. Avoiding common missteps can significantly impact the outcome and help protect your child's future. You want to make sure you're doing everything right for them.

Don't let your child speak to law enforcement without an attorney. Kids often think cooperating will help, but anything they say can be used against them. Politely inform officers that your child will not answer questions without legal counsel present. An attorney ensures their rights are protected from the start.

Avoid discussing the case with anyone other than your attorney. This includes friends, family, or even online. Loose lips can sink ships, and information shared informally can sometimes find its way back to investigators, potentially harming your child's defense.

Don't ignore court orders or deadlines. The juvenile court system has strict timelines and requirements. Missing a court date or failing to complete required actions can lead to more severe consequences, like further detention or additional charges. Stay organized and communicate with your legal team.

Avoid downplaying the seriousness of the charges. Even seemingly minor offenses can have long-term impacts on a juvenile's record, affecting future educational opportunities, employment, and even their ability to get certain licenses. Treat every charge with the gravity it deserves and seek professional legal advice.

Don't choose just any lawyer; get someone experienced in juvenile defense. The juvenile court system is specialized.
