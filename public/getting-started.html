Step 1: Understand Your Eligibility for Expungement. Not all convictions are eligible for expungement, but many are. Generally, if you successfully completed probation and paid all fines and restitution, you might qualify. This applies to many misdemeanor and some felony cases. An attorney can review your specific record and determine if your case meets the criteria, which is a crucial first step.

Step 2: Gather Necessary Documentation. The expungement process requires specific court documents related to your original conviction, such as minutes, plea agreements, and proof of probation completion. Collecting these can be time-consuming, but they are essential for filing your petition accurately.

Step 3: File Your Petition with the Court. Once eligibility is confirmed and documents are gathered, a petition for expungement (or record clearing/sealing, depending on the specific relief sought) is filed with the court where your conviction occurred. This is a formal legal process that needs to be done correctly.

Step 4: Attend Necessary Hearings. The court may schedule a hearing where you, your attorney, and the prosecutor might present arguments regarding your petition. Your attorney will represent you and advocate for your record to be cleared. This step is where skilled legal representation truly shines.

Step 5: Receive the Court’s Decision. If the court grants your petition, your plea of guilty or no contest will be withdrawn, a plea of not guilty entered, and the case dismissed. This can significantly improve your prospects for employment, housing, and more.

The entire process, from start to finish, can take several months, but the benefits are often life-changing. Step 1: The Initial Arrest and Release (Hours to Days): After being pulled over and arrested for DUI, you'll usually be booked, fingerprinted, and may have your car impounded. You might be released on bail, on your own recognizance (OR), or held until your first court date. The police will also likely confiscate your driver's license and issue you a temporary one, along with a notice of suspension from the DMV. You only have 10 days from this point to request a DMV hearing!

Step 2: The DMV Administrative Per Se Hearing Request (Within 10 Days): This is a critical, time-sensitive step. If you don't request a hearing with the California DMV within 10 days of your arrest, your license will automatically be suspended. An attorney can handle this request for you, potentially saving your driving privileges.

Step 3: Arraignment and Initial Court Appearance (Weeks to a Couple of Months): This is your first formal court appearance where you'll be informed of the charges against you and enter a plea (typically 'not guilty' at this stage). Your attorney will be there to represent you, discuss the charges, and begin negotiating with the prosecutor.

Step 4: Pre-Trial Hearings and Negotiations (Months): This phase can involve several court dates where your attorney will review evidence, file motions to suppress evidence, and engage in plea bargaining with the prosecution. Many DUI cases are resolved during this stage through a plea agreement.

Step 5: DMV Hearing (Weeks to Months after Request): If you requested one, this administrative hearing will determine if your driving privileges should be suspended by the DMV, separate from any criminal court outcomes. Your attorney can represent you here, presenting evidence to fight the suspension.
