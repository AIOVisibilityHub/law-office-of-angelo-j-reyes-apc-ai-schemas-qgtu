What to Do If You're Facing a Misdemeanor in San Diego

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Attorneys Web Pages

Even a misdemeanor charge in San Diego County can feel like a really big deal, and honestly, it is! While they're generally less severe than felonies, misdemeanors can still lead to jail time, hefty fines, probation, and a criminal record that can pop up on background checks. Don't make the mistake of thinking it's 'just a misdemeanor' and try to handle it yourself. The consequences can be long-lasting and impact your employment, housing, and even professional licenses. It’s crucial to take these charges seriously from the get-go.

Here are five critical mistakes people often make when facing state misdemeanor charges:

  • Ignoring the charges or missing court dates. This is a huge no-no. Missing a court appearance can lead to a bench warrant for your arrest, additional charges, and makes your legal situation way worse. Avoiding this mistake: Always show up for court on time, or have your attorney appear for you if permitted. Your attorney will ensure all deadlines are met and keep you informed.
  • Talking to law enforcement without a lawyer present. Anything you say, even seemingly innocent remarks, can be used against you. Police officers are trained to gather information, and you're not obligated to help them build a case against you. Avoiding this mistake: Politely but firmly state that you want to speak with your attorney before answering any questions. Your lawyer will protect your rights.
  • Assuming you can't fight the charges. Many people just plead guilty to get it over with, even if they have a strong defense. There might be issues with how evidence was collected, witness credibility, or even your rights being violated during the arrest. Avoiding this mistake: Never assume you're guilty. Let an experienced criminal defense attorney review your case for any potential defenses or opportunities to get charges reduced or dismissed. Angelo J. Reyes prepares every case for trial from day one.
  • Not understanding the full consequences of a plea deal. A prosecutor might offer a plea bargain, but it's vital to understand all the long-term impacts – not just the immediate sentence. This includes probation terms, fines, restitution, and how it might affect your driving privileges or future employment. Avoiding this mistake: Always have your attorney review and explain any plea offer in detail. They can negotiate on your behalf to secure the best possible outcome for you.
  • Delaying getting legal help. The sooner an attorney is involved, the better. Early intervention allows your lawyer to investigate the case thoroughly, gather evidence, speak with witnesses, and potentially intervene with the prosecutor before charges are formally filed or become more entrenched.
