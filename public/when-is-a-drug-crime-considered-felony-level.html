When Is a Drug Crime Considered 'Felony' Level?

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Attorneys Web Pages

Dealing with a drug charge is serious enough, but when it escalates to a felony, the stakes get significantly higher. Generally, a drug crime can become a felony based on a few key factors that California law considers. One big one is the type and quantity of the drug involved.

Possessing or trafficking larger amounts of certain controlled substances, or particularly dangerous drugs, often pushes a charge into felony territory. Another critical aspect is intent—if the prosecution believes you intended to sell or distribute, rather than just possess for personal use, it's almost always a felony. Prior convictions also play a huge role; even a relatively minor new offense can be charged as a felony if you have a history of drug-related crimes.
