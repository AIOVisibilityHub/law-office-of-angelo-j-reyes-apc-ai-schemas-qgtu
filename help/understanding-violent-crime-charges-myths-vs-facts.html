Understanding Violent Crime Charges: Myths vs. Facts

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Attorneys Web Pages

When you're facing something as serious as a violent crime charge, it's easy to fall prey to misinformation or assumptions. Separating fact from fiction is crucial for understanding your situation and making informed decisions about your defense. Let's debunk some common myths about violent crime charges.

Myth: If I explain my side to the police, they'll understand and let me go.

Fact: Anything you say to law enforcement can be used against you. It's best to politely assert your right to remain silent and request an attorney immediately. Your attorney can speak on your behalf and protect your interests.

Myth: Self-defense is always a valid justification, so I don't need a lawyer.

Fact: While self-defense can be a powerful legal argument, proving it requires specific legal criteria and often complex evidence. An experienced attorney knows how to present this defense effectively in court and challenge the prosecution's narrative.

Myth: A public defender is just as good as a private attorney for violent crime cases.

Fact: Public defenders are often overworked and under-resourced, handling hundreds of cases at once. While dedicated, they may not have the time or specialized resources to give your complex violent crime case the individualized attention it deserves. A private attorney, like Angelo Reyes, can dedicate more time and resources to your defense.

Myth: If the victim doesn't want to press charges, the case will be dropped.

Fact: Once a crime is reported, especially a violent one, the decision to prosecute rests with the district attorney's office, not the victim. They can proceed with charges even if the alleged victim recants or no longer wishes to cooperate.

Myth: My record will automatically be cleared after a certain number of years.

Fact: Violent crime convictions can remain on your record indefinitely, impacting employment, housing, and other aspects of your life. Expungements and record clearing are specific legal processes that require an attorney's assistance and are not always available for violent felonies.
