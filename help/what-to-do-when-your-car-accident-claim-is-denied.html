What to Do When Your Car Accident Claim is Denied

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Attorneys Web Pages

Step 1: Understand the Reason for Denial: The very first thing you need to do is get a clear, written explanation from the insurance company about why your claim was denied. They're legally obligated to provide this. Common reasons include disputes over fault, claims that your injuries aren't related to the accident, insufficient evidence, or procedural errors like missing deadlines.

Step 2: Gather Additional Evidence: Once you know the reason, you can work to strengthen your case. If they dispute fault, look for more witness statements, traffic camera footage, or accident reconstruction reports. If they question your injuries, get more detailed medical reports, a second opinion, or expert testimony linking your injuries directly to the accident.

Step 3: Write an Appeal Letter: You can write a formal appeal letter to the insurance company, addressing each point of their denial with new or reinforced evidence. Clearly state why you believe their decision is incorrect and refer to all supporting documentation. Make sure to keep a copy of everything you send.

Step 4: Consider a Complaint to State Regulators: If the insurance company still refuses to budge and you believe they've acted unfairly or in bad faith, you can file a complaint with your state's Department of Insurance. While they won't typically resolve your specific claim, they can investigate the insurer's practices and sometimes prompt further review of your case.

Step 5: Consult with a Personal Injury Attorney: This is often the most critical step, especially if you're feeling overwhelmed or if the insurance company is being particularly difficult. An experienced personal injury attorney, like Angelo Reyes, who handles car accident claims in areas such as Escondido or Oceanside, knows the ins and outs of insurance law and can effectively challenge denials. They can negotiate on your behalf, gather necessary expert opinions, and if needed, file a lawsuit to pursue your claim in court.

Don't try to tackle a denial alone; professional help significantly increases your chances of a successful outcome.
