Facing a Felony Charge? What to Expect Next

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Attorneys Web Pages

Being accused of a felony in California is an incredibly serious situation, carrying the potential for state prison time, hefty fines, and long-term consequences that can affect every aspect of your life. It’s completely natural to feel overwhelmed, scared, and unsure of what to do next. But understanding the typical progression of a felony case can help you prepare and realize the importance of immediate legal representation.

Attorney Angelo J. Reyes is here to help individuals throughout San Diego County navigate these challenging waters. He's trial-tested and client-focused, ensuring every case is prepared for trial from day one.

Here’s a general outline of what usually happens

  • Arrest and Booking: If you’re suspected of a felony, you'll likely be arrested, taken into custody, fingerprinted, photographed, and processed. This can be a frightening experience, but remember your right to remain silent.
  • Arraignment: This is your first court appearance. The judge will inform you of the charges, and you'll enter a plea. Bail will typically be set at this point, which can be very high for felony charges. A good defense attorney can argue for lower bail or release on your own recognizance, depending on the circumstances.
  • Preliminary Hearing: For felony cases, California law generally requires a preliminary hearing. At this hearing, a judge decides if there's enough evidence (probable cause) to believe a crime was committed and that you committed it. Your attorney can challenge the prosecution's evidence and cross-examine witnesses. This isn't a full trial, but it's a critical step where a strong defense can sometimes lead to charges being dropped or reduced.
  • Pretrial Motions and Plea Bargaining: After the preliminary hearing, your lawyer will engage in extensive investigation, evidence discovery, and file motions to challenge evidence or suppress unlawfully obtained information. This phase also includes negotiations with the prosecutor (plea bargaining) to potentially resolve the case without a trial. Angelo J. Reyes prepares every case for trial, which gives him a strong position during these negotiations.
  • Trial: If the case doesn't resolve through a plea bargain, it will go to trial. This is where your attorney will present your defense, challenge the prosecution's case, and fight for your acquittal. Given the stakes, having a seasoned trial attorney is absolutely critical.
  • Sentencing/Post-Conviction Relief: If you're convicted, the court will determine your sentence. Even after a conviction, an experienced attorney can explore options for appeals or post-conviction relief, such as expungements, record clearing, or reducing felonies to misdemeanors, to help you move forward.
