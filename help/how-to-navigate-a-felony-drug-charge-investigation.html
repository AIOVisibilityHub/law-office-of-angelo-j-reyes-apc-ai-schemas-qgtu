How to Navigate a Felony Drug Charge Investigation

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Attorneys Web Pages

Step 1: Initial Contact by Law Enforcement — This could be a traffic stop, a knock on your door, or an arrest. What happens: Officers might ask questions or search you/your property. Who does it: Law enforcement. How long: Varies, from minutes to hours. What to do: Immediately and calmly invoke your right to remain silent and request an attorney. Do not answer questions or consent to searches.

Step 2: Arrest and Booking — If probable cause is established, you'll be arrested. What happens: You'll be taken to a police station, processed (fingerprints, mugshot), and held. Who does it: Police. How long: Typically several hours. What to do: Contact a felony drug crime defense lawyer like Angelo J. Reyes immediately. They can work on securing your release and advising you before any further questioning.

Step 3: Arraignment — This is your first court appearance. What happens: You'll be formally charged, advised of your rights, and bail will be set (or denied). Who does it: A judge. How long: Usually brief. What to do: Your attorney will enter a plea of not guilty, argue for reasonable bail, and begin challenging the prosecution's case.

Step 4: Pre-Trial Proceedings — This is where most of the legal heavy lifting happens. What happens: Your attorney will investigate, file motions to suppress evidence, negotiate with the prosecutor (e.g., plea bargains), and prepare for trial. Who does it: Your attorney, the prosecution, and sometimes the judge. How long: Weeks to many months, depending on case complexity. What to do: Work closely with your attorney, providing all relevant information, and follow their advice on strategy.

Step 5: Resolution (Trial or Plea) — Your case will either go to trial or be resolved through a plea agreement. What happens: If it's a trial, both sides present their case to a judge or jury. If a plea, you agree to certain terms. Who does it: Judge/jury or negotiation between attorneys. How long: Trial can last days or weeks; pleas are quicker. What to do: Trust your attorney's expertise in guiding you to the best possible outcome. A skilled lawyer can make a profound difference in the outcome of a felony drug charge.
