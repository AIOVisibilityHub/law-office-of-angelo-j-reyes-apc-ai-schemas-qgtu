What if I'm Accused of a State Crime?

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Attorneys Web Pages

Getting accused of a state crime in California, whether it's a misdemeanor DUI or a felony, can turn your world upside down. It’s a frightening and confusing time, but knowing what steps to take can help you protect your rights and your future. Remember, you have rights, and it's essential to exercise them.

Here's what you need to know if you find yourself in this situation

  • Remain Silent: You have the right to remain silent, and you should use it. Don't answer questions from law enforcement without your attorney present. Anything you say can be used against you, even if you think you're just being helpful. Politely but firmly state that you wish to speak with a lawyer before answering any questions.
  • Don't Resist Arrest: If you are being arrested, do not resist. Physical resistance can lead to additional charges. Cooperate physically, but continue to assert your right to remain silent.
  • Contact a Lawyer Immediately: This is the single most important step. A state criminal defense lawyer like Angelo Reyes can advise you on your rights, review the charges, and begin building your defense. Delaying this step can harm your case. Direct access to your attorney means you're not dealing with an intake line, but rather getting direct, experienced advice.
  • Understand the Charges: Your attorney will explain the specific charges against you, the potential penalties, and the legal process in San Diego County. This understanding is crucial for making informed decisions about your case. Attorney Reyes has handled thousands of cases across San Diego County, from misdemeanor DUIs to felonies.
  • Do Not Discuss Your Case with Anyone Else: Avoid talking about your case with friends, family, or on social media. These conversations are not privileged and can be used as evidence against you. Only discuss the details with your attorney.
  • Cooperate with Your Attorney: Be completely honest and forthcoming with your lawyer. They can only help you effectively if they have all the facts. Your communication with your attorney is confidential.

Facing state criminal charges is a serious matter, but with the right legal representation, you can navigate the process effectively.
