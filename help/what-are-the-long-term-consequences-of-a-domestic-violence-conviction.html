What are the long-term consequences of a domestic violence conviction?

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Attorneys Web Pages

[ ] Criminal Record & Reputation: A conviction will be on your permanent criminal record, which can significantly damage your reputation in your community and professional life. It's not something that just goes away easily.

[ ] Employment Challenges: Many employers conduct background checks. A domestic violence conviction can make it extremely difficult to get or keep a job, especially in fields requiring trust or working with vulnerable populations. Even if you have a job, it could put it at risk.

[ ] Housing Difficulties: Landlords also often run background checks. Finding suitable housing can become a serious hurdle, as a conviction might be seen as a liability.

[ ] Loss of Firearm Rights: Federal law, and often state law, prohibits individuals convicted of domestic violence from owning or possessing firearms. This is a permanent loss that affects many aspects of life, including certain professions and self-defense.

[ ] Impact on Family & Custody: A domestic violence conviction can severely affect child custody and visitation rights. Courts prioritize the safety of children, and such a conviction can be a major factor in decisions regarding parental roles.

[ ] Mandatory Programs & Fines: You'll likely face mandatory domestic violence offender programs, which are lengthy and often costly. There will also be substantial fines and court fees.

[ ] Immigration Consequences: For non-citizens, a domestic violence conviction can have severe immigration consequences, potentially leading to deportation or making it impossible to obtain a green card or citizenship.

[ ] Restraining Orders: Often, a conviction comes with protective orders or restraining orders, which can restrict your contact with certain individuals and force you to stay a certain distance away from them or specific locations.
