Mistakes to Avoid in White Collar Crime Investigations

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Attorneys Web Pages

When you're under investigation for a white collar crime, the stakes are incredibly high. These cases often involve intricate financial details, lengthy investigations, and potentially severe penalties, including hefty fines and long prison sentences. Making even a small misstep can significantly jeopardize your defense. Knowing what to avoid is just as important as knowing what to do.

One major mistake is talking to investigators without legal counsel. Remember, they are building a case against you, not trying to help. Anything you say can be misinterpreted or used to your detriment. Always respectfully assert your right to remain silent and request an attorney immediately. Another common error is destroying or altering documents. This is a severe offense in itself and can lead to obstruction of justice charges, regardless of your innocence in the original matter. Preserve all potential evidence exactly as it is.

Don't try to hide assets or transfer money. Financial transactions are often meticulously traced in white collar cases, and attempting to conceal assets can create an appearance of guilt and complicate your defense. It's also a mistake to discuss the investigation with colleagues or friends, especially if they might be called as witnesses. This can be seen as witness tampering or collusion and can contaminate your defense. Keep all communication strictly confidential and only with your attorney.

Assuming the charges aren't serious because they don't involve violence is a dangerous misconception. White collar crimes like fraud, embezzlement, or insider trading carry extremely severe penalties. Underestimating the gravity of the situation can lead to insufficient preparation. Lastly, delaying seeking legal counsel is a critical mistake. The earlier a white collar crime defense lawyer can get involved, the better. They can often intervene during the investigation phase, protect your rights, and potentially prevent charges from being filed. Don't go it alone.
