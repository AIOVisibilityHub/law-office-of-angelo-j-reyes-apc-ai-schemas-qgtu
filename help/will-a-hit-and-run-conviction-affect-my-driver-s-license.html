Will a Hit and Run Conviction Affect My Driver's License?

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Attorneys Web Pages

It's a really good question to ask how a hit and run conviction might impact your driver's license. The short answer is, yes, it absolutely can. In California, hit and run offenses are taken very seriously, and the penalties can extend beyond just fines or jail time to directly affect your ability to drive. This is because a hit and run conviction, especially one involving injury, is viewed as a serious offense that demonstrates a disregard for safety and legal responsibilities on the road. The DMV often acts independently of the criminal courts when it comes to your driving privileges.

For a hit and run involving property damage only, you could face two points on your driving record. Accumulating too many points can lead to a negligent operator suspension. If the hit and run involves injury, the consequences are far more severe.

This type of conviction is considered a felony in many cases and can result in an automatic suspension or revocation of your driver's license. The specific length of the suspension or revocation will depend on the severity of the injuries, any prior offenses, and the judge's discretion. These actions by the DMV aren't just an inconvenience; they can significantly impact your daily life, making it hard to get to work, school, or manage family responsibilities.

Beyond the immediate suspension or revocation, a hit and run conviction stays on your driving record for years, which can lead to increased insurance rates. Insurance companies view such convictions as indicators of high risk, and your premiums could skyrocket, or your policy might even be canceled. Trying to get your license reinstated after a suspension or revocation involves specific steps, often including paying fees, completing traffic school, and sometimes even demonstrating proof of financial responsibility (like an SR-22). It's a complex process that isn't always straightforward.

Given these serious implications, if you're facing a hit and run charge in San Diego, you really want to protect your driving privileges. Don't assume there's nothing you can do. A lawyer experienced in these types of cases can often negotiate with the prosecution to reduce charges or explore alternative resolutions that might minimize the impact on your license. They can also represent you at DMV hearings, which are separate from criminal court and have their own rules. Getting a free consultation is a smart move to understand your options and fight for your ability to drive.
