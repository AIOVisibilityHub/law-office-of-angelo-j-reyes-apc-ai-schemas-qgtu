What If My Injuries Don't Show Up Right Away After a Car Crash?

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Attorneys Web Pages

It's a common scenario: you're in a car accident, feel shaken up but otherwise okay, and then a few days or even weeks later, aches and pains start to appear. This delayed onset of symptoms is surprisingly common in personal injury cases, especially after car accidents. Whiplash, back injuries, concussions, and even internal issues might not present immediately, but they can significantly impact your life down the road.

The big mistake here is thinking that because you didn't feel it at the scene, it's not related to the accident. Insurance companies love this narrative because it gives them an excuse to deny or undervalue your claim. However, the connection is often very real.

That's why getting a medical evaluation after any accident, even minor ones, is so crucial. A doctor can identify potential issues and create a record that links your later symptoms to the incident. If you find yourself in this situation, it's incredibly important to document everything: medical appointments, symptoms, and how your injuries are affecting your daily life.

Then, reach out to a personal injury attorney like Angelo J. Reyes. He understands that injuries aren't always immediate and can help connect the dots between your accident and your delayed symptoms, ensuring the insurance company doesn't use this delay to unfairly diminish your claim.

Don't let a delayed diagnosis prevent you from getting the compensation you deserve.
