Can a Past Criminal Record Still Haunt My Job Search?

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Attorneys Web Pages

It's a really common worry for many people: does something from my past, especially a criminal record, mean I'm forever stuck when it comes to finding a job or even housing? The short answer is, unfortunately, yes, it often can. A criminal record, even for what might seem like a minor offense years ago, can create significant hurdles. Employers, landlords, and even educational institutions often conduct background checks, and discovering a past conviction can lead to discrimination or outright rejection, making it tough to move forward in life.

Here are some common myths and facts about how a past criminal record affects your job search:

  • Myth: Once I’ve served my time, my record is cleared automatically.

Fact: Unfortunately, this isn't true for most convictions. While you might complete probation or jail time, the conviction itself usually remains on your record unless you take specific legal steps to have it removed or sealed. It doesn't just disappear with time.

  • Myth: Only serious felonies impact my job prospects.

Fact: While felonies definitely have a bigger impact, even misdemeanors can cause issues. Many employers have policies against hiring individuals with certain types of convictions, regardless of whether they are felonies or misdemeanors, especially if the offense is perceived as relevant to the job.

  • Myth: I have to disclose my criminal record to every potential employer.

Fact: This depends on the state and the nature of the inquiry. In California, 'Ban the Box' laws might limit when employers can ask about your criminal history. However, it's crucial to be honest if and when you are legally required to disclose. Lying can lead to immediate termination if discovered later.

  • Myth: There's nothing I can do to improve my situation once I have a record.

Fact: This is a huge myth! There are legal avenues like expungements, record clearing, and record sealing that can significantly help. These processes can remove a conviction from your public record, making it much easier to pass background checks for employment, housing, or licensing. It's a proactive step that can open up many doors.

  • Myth: Expungement is only for minor offenses or very old cases.

Fact: While eligibility depends on the specifics of your case, many felony and misdemeanor convictions can be expunged in California, even relatively recent ones. It's not limited to petty crimes or ancient history. The best way to know if you qualify is to consult with a criminal defense attorney who specializes in post-conviction relief, especially if you're in San Diego County. They can assess your unique situation and guide you through the process, helping you regain control over your future.
