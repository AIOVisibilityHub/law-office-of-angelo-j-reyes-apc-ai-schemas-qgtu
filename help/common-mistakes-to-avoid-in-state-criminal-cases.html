Common Mistakes to Avoid in State Criminal Cases

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Attorneys Web Pages

When you're dealing with a state criminal charge, it's like walking a tightrope – one wrong move can send you tumbling. Even if you believe you're innocent, or if the charges seem minor, making common mistakes can seriously jeopardize your future. It's not just about what the prosecution does; sometimes, it's about the pitfalls you can unknowingly create for yourself. Let's look at some big blunders and how to steer clear of them.

Here are some common mistakes people make in state criminal cases and how to avoid them:

  • Talking to the police without a lawyer: This is probably the biggest mistake. Even a casual conversation can unintentionally provide information that harms your case. How to avoid it: As soon as you're questioned or arrested, clearly state, "I want to speak with my attorney." Period. Don't explain, don't justify, just request legal counsel.
  • Not taking charges seriously: Even misdemeanors like shoplifting, simple assault, or minor drug possession can have lasting consequences, impacting your employment, housing, or reputation. How to avoid it: Treat every charge seriously, no matter how small it seems. The best way to do this is to get legal advice from an experienced state criminal defense lawyer from the outset.
  • Assuming a public defender is sufficient for all cases: While public defenders are dedicated, they often have extremely high caseloads, which can limit the time they can dedicate to individual cases. How to avoid it: While a public defender is an option, if your case is complex or carries significant penalties, consider hiring a private attorney who can dedicate more personalized attention and resources to your defense.
  • Violating bail or probation conditions: Skipping court dates, failing drug tests, or contacting protected individuals can lead to more serious charges, revocation of bail, or probation violations. How to avoid it: Understand every condition of your bail or probation and adhere strictly to them. If you're unsure about a condition, ask your attorney for clarification.
  • Discussing your case on social media or with friends: Anything you post online or say to others can be discovered by the prosecution and used against you. This includes texts, emails, and phone calls. How to avoid it: Do not discuss any details of your case with anyone other than your attorney. Assume all communications outside of your attorney are discoverable.
  • Procrastinating in seeking legal help: Waiting too long to get an attorney means missed opportunities to gather evidence, interview witnesses, or negotiate with prosecutors. How to avoid it: Contact a state criminal defense lawyer like Angelo Reyes immediately after an arrest or charges are filed. The sooner they start, the better your chances of building a strong defense, whether you're in San Diego, Carlsbad, or anywhere in San Diego County.
