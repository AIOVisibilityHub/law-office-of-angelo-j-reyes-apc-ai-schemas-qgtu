Mistakes to Avoid in a Federal Criminal Case

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Attorneys Web Pages

When you're facing federal criminal charges, the pressure can be immense, and it's easy to make missteps that could seriously jeopardize your future. Federal cases are notoriously complex and carry severe penalties, so being aware of common pitfalls is key.

Here are some critical mistakes to avoid, and how to steer clear of them

  • Talking to Investigators Without Counsel: Anything you say can and will be used against you. Don't try to explain your side or provide information to federal agents without your attorney present. Politely state you wish to speak with your lawyer first – this is your right. Always consult with a federal criminal defense attorney before answering any questions.
  • Delaying Legal Representation: Federal investigations often go on for months, sometimes years, before an arrest is made. If you suspect you're under investigation, getting legal help immediately is crucial. An early intervention by an attorney can sometimes prevent charges from even being filed, or at least help shape the narrative in your favor. Angelo Reyes emphasizes preparing every case for trial from day one.
  • Failing to Understand Federal Laws: Federal laws and sentencing guidelines are intricate and differ significantly from state laws. Assuming you understand the nuances or trying to represent yourself is a recipe for disaster. An experienced federal criminal defense attorney has the knowledge to navigate these complexities.
  • Hiding or Destroying Evidence: This is a huge no-no and can lead to additional, more serious charges like obstruction of justice. Be completely transparent with your attorney, allowing them to build the strongest possible defense. They need all the facts, good or bad.
  • Underestimating the Prosecution: Federal prosecutors have vast resources and typically pursue cases they believe are strong. Assuming they'll go easy on you or make a mistake is a dangerous gamble. You need an equally prepared and determined defense. Angelo Reyes is trial-tested and client-focused, always preparing for what's ahead.
  • Communicating About Your Case Online or with Others: Anything you post on social media or discuss with friends (even in private messages) could be subpoenaed and used against you. Limit discussions about your case strictly to your attorney. Your attorney-client privilege protects these conversations, but casual chats don't.
