Mistakes to Avoid After Your Car's Been Damaged

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Attorneys Web Pages

When your car gets damaged in an accident, it's easy to make missteps that could hurt your claim. Insurance companies are quick to act, often trying to settle things before you even know the full extent of your losses. Avoiding common mistakes can help protect your rights and ensure you get fair compensation for your vehicle and other property.

Don't delay reporting the accident. Waiting too long can make it harder to prove what happened and may even violate your insurance policy terms. Notify your own insurance company as soon as reasonably possible, even if the other driver was at fault.

Avoid giving recorded statements to the other driver's insurance company without guidance. Their adjusters are trained to ask questions in a way that can get you to say something that undermines your claim. Stick to the facts and don't speculate about fault or the extent of damage beyond what is immediately obvious.

Don't accept the first settlement offer too quickly. Initial offers are almost always low, designed to get you to close the claim cheaply. Do your research on the value of your damaged vehicle and consider all your losses, not just the repair cost or book value. This includes rental car fees, towing, and diminished value.

Never admit fault at the scene of an accident. Even if you think you were partly to blame, discussing fault can be used against you. Simply exchange information, check on others, and call the police if necessary. Let the investigation determine fault.

Failing to document the scene thoroughly is another big mistake. Take clear photos and videos of all vehicle damage, the accident scene, road conditions, and any visible injuries. Gather contact info for witnesses and all drivers involved. This documentation is invaluable for your claim.
