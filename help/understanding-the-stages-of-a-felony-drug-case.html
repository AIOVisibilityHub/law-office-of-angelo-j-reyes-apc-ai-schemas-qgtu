Understanding the Stages of a Felony Drug Case

Home About Services Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Attorneys Web Pages

Step 1: Arrest and Initial Custody. This is where it all begins. You're taken into custody, booked, and processed. It's absolutely critical here to remember your right to remain silent and to request an attorney immediately. Don't make any statements without legal counsel present. This phase typically involves fingerprinting and background checks, lasting a few hours.

Step 2: Arraignment and Bail Hearing. Your first court appearance, often within a few days of arrest if you're in custody. The charges are formally read, and you'll enter a plea. The judge will also consider setting bail, which is crucial for your temporary freedom while the case proceeds. Your attorney can argue for a reasonable bail amount or release on your own recognizance.

Step 3: Preliminary Hearing. In felony cases, this hearing is vital. The prosecution presents enough evidence to convince a judge that there's probable cause to believe a crime was committed and you committed it. Your attorney can cross-examine witnesses and challenge evidence. If probable cause is found, your case moves forward; if not, it could be dismissed. This can happen weeks or a couple of months after arraignment.

Step 4: Pre-Trial Motions and Negotiations. This stage involves your attorney filing motions to suppress evidence, challenge procedures, or seek discovery. There will also be extensive negotiations with the prosecutor, exploring plea bargains or diversion programs. This is where much of the behind-the-scenes work happens, and it can take several months depending on the case's complexity.

Step 5: Trial (if no resolution). If plea negotiations don't yield a satisfactory outcome, the case proceeds to trial. A jury or judge will hear all the evidence and arguments before delivering a verdict. A felony trial can be lengthy, sometimes lasting weeks, and it requires meticulous preparation and strong advocacy. Your lawyer's experience here is invaluable.
